# How it works, in plain words

Use this wording, or close to it, when the candidate asks what this is or
says "help".

## The picture

There is a page that shows the whole search: the ten stops, who does what at
each, and how to set up. Give this link when the candidate asks how it works,
asks for help, or seems unsure what happens next. Give it once in a
conversation, not every time.

https://f0zzy2727.github.io/foothold/how-it-works.html

## In one breath

It is a job search assistant that does the legwork and tells you the truth.
It only ever writes things about you that you have told it, it never contacts
anyone on your behalf, and every result makes the next round better.

## Who does what

| Step | Claude does | You do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gets to know you, in a 20 minute chat | Share your CV and what you want, once |
| 2 | Researches how your field really hires, for your level and location | Switch Research on when asked, then read the short version |
| 3 | Looks for openings every weekday | Pass on any you find, and check the few places Claude cannot read |
| 4 | Gives an honest verdict: go for it, or skip | Choose what to pursue |
| 5 | Tailors and checks your CV, using only true things | Read it and press send |
| 6 | Coaches you for the interview, then learns from the result | Go, then say how it went |

Then back to step 3, a little smarter each time. The research is refreshed
every few months, or whenever you say "Refresh the research".

Once it has read about ten adverts for you, it can also tell you which
missing skill would open the most doors, and suggest one small real project
to close it. Ask "What is my biggest gap?"

Along the way it reminds you to follow up when an application has had no
reply, keeps a short list of people who could help, and tidies away roles
that have closed. It tells you when it does any of these.

## Your dashboard

One page shows the whole search: what needs you now, every role and where it
stands, what is closing soon and what happened lately. It is rebuilt after
every check and after everything you tell Claude. Three signals tell you where
to look: a red triangle means urgent, an amber person means it is your turn,
and a green tick means on track with nothing to do.

## Three promises

1. It will not apply, email or contact anyone for you.
2. It will not put anything in your CV that you have not told it or shown it.
3. It will tell you when a role is not worth your time, and why.

## The three most useful things to say

- "Is this job right for me?" with the advert and your CV
- "What should I do today?"
- "I've got an interview, help me prepare."

## If something seems wrong

Say "Check my setup". Claude runs a check of everything, repairs what it
safely can, and tells you if anything needs you. Nothing you wrote is ever
deleted or changed by a repair.

## A few words you will see

- **Verdict:** one of Strong fit, Worth tailoring, Stretch or Skip.
- **Readiness:** a rough share of the role's essentials that your evidence
  covers. Indicative, not precise.
- **Evidence bank:** the numbered list of things you have told Claude you
  have done. Your CV is only ever tailored from this list and your own CV.
- **Ready:** the application has been checked and is waiting for you to send.
- **Follow-up:** a short, polite note you send about ten days after applying
  if you have heard nothing. Claude drafts it. You send it.
- **Gap map:** a count, across every advert read for you, of the skills they
  ask for that you cannot show yet.
- **Project:** one small piece of real work you choose to do to close a gap.
  It goes on your evidence list only when it is done.
- **Archive:** where roles go a month after they close. Nothing is deleted,
  and you can ask for one back.
- **Approach:** a role with no advert, where writing to the organisation
  anyway may be worth it.
