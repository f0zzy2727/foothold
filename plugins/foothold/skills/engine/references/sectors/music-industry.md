# Sector pack: music industry

This pack tells the researcher and the scout what is particular about this
sector. It contains questions and working hypotheses, not findings. The
researcher must test each one for the candidate's segments and locations and
report what the evidence says, including where a hypothesis is wrong.

## Segments to map

Narrow to the segments named in `profile/target.md`, but place them in the
whole picture:

- Recorded music (labels, distribution, catalogue)
- Music publishing and rights (publishers, collection societies, sync and
  licensing)
- Live (promoters, venues, festivals, booking agencies, touring and
  production crews, ticketing)
- Artist management and artist services
- Studios, production and audio engineering
- Streaming platforms and music technology companies
- Marketing, PR, radio, media and content
- Instruments, equipment and retail
- Education, community music, funded and public bodies

## Working hypotheses to test

1. A meaningful share of roles is filled through contacts, referrals and
   prior freelance or voluntary work, without an advert.
2. Proof of involvement (credits, events worked, releases, a portfolio) weighs
   as much as formal qualifications for many roles.
3. Entry routes are often informal: internships, assistant and runner roles,
   street teams, volunteering at festivals and venues, student or community
   radio.
4. Hiring in live music is seasonal.
5. Small employers hire without applicant tracking software; large ones use it.
6. Freelance and short contracts are common, and pay at entry level is low
   relative to comparable roles elsewhere.

For each: supported, partly supported or not supported, with sources.

## Sector-specific research questions

- Which organisations in the target locations employ people in the target
  segments, and roughly how many?
- Where are roles actually advertised: trade bodies, sector job boards,
  company pages, newsletters, social channels, mailing lists?
- Which trade bodies, unions, funds and schemes exist for people entering the
  sector, and what do they offer?
- Which events, conferences and showcases matter for meeting people in the
  target segments, and when are they?
- What does a credible portfolio or proof of involvement look like for each
  target role?
- What are realistic pay ranges and contract norms at the candidate's level?
- What is the hiring calendar across a year?
- Which stepping-stone roles most often lead to the target roles?
- What vocabulary and keywords does the sector use for the target roles?

## Red flags specific to this sector

The scout and the spec analyst flag these prominently:

- Unpaid roles described as exposure or experience, outside a recognised
  scheme
- Pay-to-play or pay-to-work arrangements, including fees for showcases,
  placements or representation
- Vague "talent scout" or "A&R" roles paid by commission only
- Requests for money, bank details or identity documents before an offer
- Roles with no named organisation or no verifiable track record

## Approach path

If hypothesis 1 is supported, the researcher builds `research/targets.md` with
care: for each organisation, what it does, why it fits the candidate, the
function or role title to approach (not private individuals' personal
details), and any current signal that makes now a good moment.

## Evidence the intake should ask about

Events worked or organised, releases or credits, tools and software used,
promotion or social channels run, radio or press work, volunteering, any paid
work in or near the sector, and who the candidate already knows.
